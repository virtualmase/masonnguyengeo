The strategic context

The SERP changed. The infrastructure didn't.

In 2025, AI Overviews appear above the fold for the majority of informational queries on Google. Zero-click rates — queries that resolve without a user visiting any linked page — now exceed 60% across informational and navigational intent. For AI-first brands whose audiences ask process questions, definition questions, and comparison questions, this is not a future concern. It is the current state of the traffic they were counting on.

The obvious response — abandon SEO, go all-in on GEO — misunderstands the structure of the problem. The signals that Google uses to decide which sources to cite inside an AI Overview are substantially the same signals that determine organic rank: domain trust, topical authority, crawlable content, and E-E-A-T evidence. Dismantling the SEO foundation to rebuild a GEO-only strategy discards the infrastructure that feeds both.

The correct response is architectural. SEO and GEO share an infrastructure layer — content quality, domain health, citation networks, and structured data — and diverge on their objective layers. Running them in parallel means building the shared infrastructure once and orienting the objective layer toward both rank position and citation frequency simultaneously. That is what this guide is for.

"Zero-click is not the death of SEO. It is the expansion of what SEO needs to accomplish. The goal was never traffic — it was presence. AI Overviews are presence."

Zero-click strategy

Zero-click is a positioning strategy, not a traffic problem

Zero-click SEO describes the practice of optimizing for SERP features — AI Overviews, featured snippets, People Also Ask boxes, knowledge panels — that answer queries directly on the results page. These features reduce click-through rates to individual pages. They also dramatically increase brand impression frequency among people who will never visit your site.

For an AI-first brand, the frame shift is critical. If your goal is traffic volume, zero-click features are adversarial — they intercept users before they reach you. If your goal is authority positioning, zero-click features are the most efficient brand presence tool available: your entity name appears in front of every person who searched the query, attributed to your domain, without requiring them to click anywhere.

The content strategy implication is direct. Pages optimized to capture zero-click features lead with direct answers. They do not bury the answer behind context paragraphs or save the conclusion for the end. The first 50 to 100 words of each section answer the question the section's heading posed. This is also — not coincidentally — the format that LLM retrieval systems extract most reliably. Zero-click optimization and GEO optimization require the same content structure.

Content architecture

Three content strategies for AI-first brands

AI-first brands need content that works across three distinct surfaces simultaneously: traditional search rankings, AI Overview citations, and standalone LLM answer citation. Each surface has slightly different format preferences, but all three draw from the same content infrastructure.

Strategy A · Rank-first
Content that earns and holds organic positions
Pages built for comprehensive topical coverage, keyword intent satisfaction, and acquisition of editorial backlinks. These pages anchor your topical authority and feed the domain trust signals that flow downstream to GEO performance.
Pillar pages covering full concept clusters
Comparison and "versus" content with clear verdicts
Data-backed original research others will cite
Long-form how-to guides with HowTo schema
Strategy B · Bridge
Content optimized for both rank and AI Overview citation
The highest-leverage content type for AI-first brands. Written with answer-layer leads, FAQPage schema, and direct-quotable section openings. Designed to rank organically and be cited in AI Overviews for the same queries simultaneously.

AI Overviews optimization

How to optimize content for Google AI Overviews

Google AI Overviews operate on a hybrid retrieval model — they draw from Google's existing Search index, weighting pages that already rank for the query, and apply additional scoring for structured data completeness, E-E-A-T signals, and content formatting. Being cited in an AI Overview does not require ranking first; it requires meeting a structured data and content format threshold that the Overview system can extract from.

The content format that AI Overviews extract from most reliably is what GEO practitioners call the answer-layer format: a direct, quotable statement in the first sentence of the section, followed by elaboration, evidence, or nuance. The Overview system extracts the lead, not the elaboration. A section that buries its core claim in the third paragraph is rarely cited regardless of its ranking position.

E-E-A-T signals — Evidence of Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trust — are the primary differentiator between pages at similar rank positions that do and do not appear in AI Overviews. First-person experience language, named authorship with schema attribution, dated publication, and explicit credentials are not decoration. They are the E-E-A-T signals the AI Overview system weights when deciding which source is most authoritative for a given extraction.

Dual-purpose
Answer-layer section leads
Direct answer in the first sentence of every section. Write as if the section heading is the question and the first sentence is a standalone answer that will be read without any context. SEO benefit: featured snippet eligibility. GEO benefit: LLM extraction accuracy.
Dual-purpose
FAQPage schema with complete answers
Each FAQ entry should be a complete, standalone answer — 40 to 80 words, no pronouns requiring page context to resolve. Implemented with FAQPage schema. SEO benefit: People Also Ask eligibility. GEO benefit: Direct query-to-answer extraction.
GEO-specific
Full entity naming in every section
Use the full entity name — not a pronoun, not "the company," not "we" — on first reference in every section. LLM retrievers process sections independently. An entity that is only named in the introduction is invisible in extracted sections.
SEO-weighted
Explicit first-person experience signals
E-E-A-T Experience signals require language that demonstrates the author actually did the thing being described. "In our testing," "I implemented this for a client in 2024," and similar phrases are not filler — they are the trust signals that distinguish AI Overview citations from excluded sources.
Implementation order

Priority order for AI-first brand SEO/GEO integration

Every implementation begins with the shared infrastructure. Trying to build GEO performance on a broken SEO foundation is building without a floor. The shared infrastructure comes first; the discipline-specific layers follow.

01
Canonical URL architecture — one stable, semantic URL per concept
Every content page lives at a persistent, descriptive URL with no parameter strings. The canonical tag points to itself. This is the base requirement for both Google's ranking algorithm and LLM retrieval systems, both of which dereference URLs when assessing source reliability.
SEO + GEO
02
Entity anchor markup — Person or Organization schema on every page
Schema.org Person or Organization markup with a complete sameAs array, deployed on the homepage and About page as a minimum. This is the entity foundation that both Google Knowledge Graph and LLM knowledge bases reference when resolving your entity against a query.
SEO + GEO
03
Article and author schema on all long-form content
Article schema with author, datePublished, dateModified, and mainEntityOfPage on every content page. The author field must reference the Person schema entity via @id, not a plain text name string. This establishes the E-E-A-T authorship chain that AI Overviews and LLM retrievers both use.
SEO + GEO
04
FAQPage schema on all pillar and bridge content
Every pillar page and bridge content page should contain a minimum of four FAQ entries with complete standalone answers. FAQPage schema is among the most LLM-legible structured data formats and the primary enabler of People Also Ask and AI Overview extraction simultaneously.
SEO + GEO
05
Wikidata entity registration
Create or claim a Wikidata entry for the brand and its principals. Include the canonical URL, external identifier references, and a structured description. Reference the Wikidata Q-number in the sameAs array of your schema markup. This is a pure GEO signal with no direct SEO effect, but it is the entry point for knowledge graph recognition across LLM retrieval systems.
GEO
06
Answer-layer content audit and reformatting
Audit the top 10 highest-traffic content pages. For each, ensure the first sentence of every section answers the section heading's implicit question directly. Rewrite any section that buries its answer. Update dateModified in schema. This single structural change is responsible for a measurable share of AI Overview citation lift in every implementation tested.
SEO + GEO
07
Structured editorial citation program
Secure four to six editorial placements per quarter in publications indexed by Perplexity and Google Search. Each placement must name the brand entity explicitly, link to the canonical URL, and include an entity description consistent with the schema markup. This feeds both domain authority (SEO) and citation network density (GEO).

Common questions

SEO for AI brands — frequently asked questions

Should AI-first brands still invest in traditional SEO?
Yes, with a repositioned goal. Traditional SEO no longer delivers traffic the way it did before AI Overviews and zero-click results absorbed the top of the SERP. But the signals that traditional SEO builds — domain authority, topical coverage depth, crawlable content architecture, and backlink profiles — are exactly the signals that Google's AI Overview system uses to decide which sources to cite. Investing in SEO for AI-first brands means optimizing for citation eligibility, not just rank position.
What is zero-click SEO and how does it change content strategy?
Zero-click SEO is the practice of optimizing content to appear in SERP features — featured snippets, AI Overviews, People Also Ask boxes — that answer queries directly on the search results page without the user clicking through to any site. For AI-first brands, zero-click is not a threat but a positioning opportunity: appearing in an AI Overview or featured snippet builds brand authority in the AI-indexed view of your domain, even when it does not drive direct traffic. The content strategy shift is toward answer-layer formatting — leading each section with a direct, quotable claim — rather than engagement-layer formatting that buries the answer to encourage time-on-page.
How do Google AI Overviews decide which sources to cite?
Google AI Overviews draw on a combination of traditional Search ranking signals and additional structured data signals. Pages that already rank in the top 10 for a query have a higher baseline probability of being cited in an AI Overview for that query. Beyond rank, AI Overviews prioritize pages with complete structured data markup (particularly FAQPage, HowTo, and Article schema), strong E-E-A-T signals (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness), direct-answer formatting in the first paragraph, and consistent entity attribution. A page can be cited in an AI Overview while ranking #6 organically if its schema and content structure outperform higher-ranking pages on these dimensions.
What signals transfer from SEO to GEO?
The strongest transfer signals are topical authority (content depth and breadth in a defined domain), domain trust (crawlability, canonical consistency, and lack of spam signals), backlink quality (editorial citations from relevant, authoritative sources), and content freshness (regular dateModified updates paired with substantive revisions). The signals that do not transfer well — keyword density, meta description optimization, and internal anchor text ratios — matter primarily for rank position and have little direct effect on LLM citation frequency.
Is SGE the same as Google AI Overviews?
SGE (Search Generative Experience) was Google's original internal name for the feature that launched publicly as AI Overviews in May 2024. The terms are used interchangeably in older documentation and industry writing. For content optimization purposes they are the same system, and all SGE optimization guidance applies directly to AI Overviews. The content architecture requirements — answer-layer formatting, complete schema markup, E-E-A-T signals, and topical authority — have not substantively changed between the SGE beta and the AI Overviews launch.
