The methodology

Why rankings alone no longer measure presence

A brand can hold the first organic position for a competitive keyword and be completely absent from the AI-generated summary above it. That gap is not a penalty. It is a measurement problem: the metric used to declare success — rank — no longer captures the surface where decisions are made.

The ARM Framework was built to close that gap. It gives brands a single coherent system for measuring and improving what actually drives citation frequency in large language model outputs: the strength of entity recognition, the precision of topical alignment, and the velocity of signal propagation into retrieval pipelines.

Each pillar maps to a distinct failure mode. Brands missing from AI answers are typically suffering from one of three conditions: low authority (the model cannot confidently resolve the entity), low relevance (the content does not map cleanly to query intent at the concept level), or low momentum (signal freshness has decayed below retrieval thresholds). ARM diagnoses which pillar is the primary constraint and builds from there.

Pillar one — Authority
A
Machine trust layer

Authority: What the model believes about your entity before the query arrives

Authority in the ARM Framework is not domain authority in the traditional sense. It is the confidence score a language model assigns to your entity when resolving an ambiguous reference. When a user asks ChatGPT for "a GEO consultant," the model must resolve that category against a set of known entities. Brands with high ARM authority are resolved quickly and confidently. Brands with low authority are either absent or confused with adjacent entities.

Building authority requires three concurrent tracks. The first is entity registration: ensuring your brand, principals, and products have structured, machine-readable records across knowledge graphs (Wikidata, Google Knowledge Graph API), schema.org markup, and canonical entity anchors. The second is citation network depth — the number and quality of third-party sources that reference your entity in unambiguous, attributable ways. The third is disambiguation hygiene: removing conflicting or duplicate entity signals that cause retrieval systems to hedge or omit.

Authority is the slowest pillar to build and the longest to maintain. It compounds over time. A brand that establishes clean entity signals in 2025 holds a structural advantage that cannot be copied in a single content sprint.

Wikidata entity record — structured, cited, linked to known identifiers across multiple language editions
Schema.org Person / Organization markup — sameAs arrays pointing to LinkedIn, Crunchbase, official social profiles
Third-party citation network — editorial mentions in topically relevant publications with consistent entity naming
Disambiguation files — geo.txt / entity anchor pages that resolve ambiguous name collisions
Canonical URL structure — persistent, consistent URLs that retrieval systems can reliably dereference over time
Pillar two — Relevance
R
Semantic alignment layer

Relevance: The precision of fit between your content and AI query intent

Relevance in the ARM context is semantic alignment, not keyword proximity. An LLM answering a question about generative engine optimization does not match keywords against a document. It resolves the query to a concept space and finds the sources whose coverage most completely and confidently inhabits that space. A page optimized for the keyword "GEO strategy" but structured as a listicle provides weak coverage of the underlying concept cluster. A page that maps the relationships between entities, processes, and outcomes in the GEO domain provides strong coverage — and earns citation.

Building relevance requires content to be organized at the concept level, not the page level. Topic clusters alone are insufficient; the relationships between concepts must be made explicit and machine-readable. FAQ schema, How-To schema, and definition blocks are not just SEO decoration — they are concept-level signals that retrieval systems use to map your content to query intent.

Relevance is also the pillar most sensitive to topical drift. A brand that earns strong relevance signals in one domain and then publishes generalist content risks diluting its concept footprint. Discipline about topical focus is a relevance strategy.

Concept-level content architecture — pages that own a concept cluster, not just a keyword, with explicit relationship mapping
FAQ and definition schema — machine-readable Q&A blocks that LLMs can resolve directly against query intent
Topical fence discipline — deliberate restriction of content to a defined domain to maintain signal concentration
Semantic entity interlinking — explicit named-entity references within content that extend the brand's knowledge graph surface
Answer-layer formatting — direct, quotable prose in the first 100 words of each section, structured for LLM extraction
Pillar three — Momentum
M
Signal velocity layer

Momentum: The velocity of fresh signals entering retrieval pipelines

Momentum is the most misunderstood pillar. It is frequently conflated with content volume — the mistaken belief that publishing more is publishing better. Momentum is not about output quantity. It is about signal propagation velocity: the rate at which fresh, attributable signals about your entity and your topics reach the inputs that training and retrieval systems consume.

A single well-structured piece that earns three editorial citations in high-authority publications generates more momentum than twenty blog posts that circulate only within the brand's own channels. Momentum is the compounding product of publication quality, syndication reach, and citation attribution speed.

Momentum also decays. A brand that published strong GEO content in 2023 and went quiet in 2025 may find its citation frequency declining — not because the content degraded, but because newer, fresher signals from competitors have displaced it in retrieval rankings. Maintaining momentum requires a systematic publishing cadence calibrated to retrieval decay curves, not editorial convenience.

Editorial syndication cadence — structured placement of attributed content in third-party publications on a defined schedule
Citation attribution speed — the time between publication and first third-party reference, which affects retrieval indexing priority
Canonical freshness signals — regular dateModified schema updates paired with substantive content revisions on high-authority pages
Social proof velocity — measurable engagement on content that corroborates authority signals already present in knowledge graphs
Training data eligibility — content published in formats and on domains that LLM data pipelines are known to prioritize during periodic updates
Implementation

How an ARM engagement runs

Every engagement follows the same diagnostic-first sequence. No implementation begins before the audit reveals which pillar is the primary constraint.

01
Entity audit — map the current machine-readable footprint
Structured review of existing schema markup, knowledge graph records, third-party citation network, and entity disambiguation status across major LLM retrieval sources.
02
Pillar diagnosis — identify the primary constraint
ARM scoring across all three pillars. A single score reveals which pillar is limiting citation performance and sets the intervention priority for the first 90 days.
03
Authority foundation — register and disambiguate the entity
Wikidata setup, schema.org implementation, sameAs array construction, disambiguation file creation, and canonical URL architecture. This runs in parallel with content work.
04
Content architecture — build the concept-level topic map
Pillar pages and cluster pages structured for LLM extraction. Every page follows the ARM content template: answer-layer lead, concept-level body, FAQ schema, entity interlinking.
05
Momentum program — cadenced syndication and citation building
Structured editorial placements in topically relevant publications. Citation targets identified by retrieval-source analysis, not domain authority metrics.
06
Measurement — track citation frequency, not rank position
Monthly ARM scorecard across all three pillars. Citation frequency in LLM outputs measured by systematic prompt testing across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, and Gemini.
Common questions

ARM Framework — frequently asked questions

How is the ARM Framework different from traditional topical authority SEO?
Topical authority SEO optimizes for ranking signals — the combination of backlinks, content depth, and crawl signals that influence a search engine's ranking algorithm. ARM optimizes for citation signals — the combination of entity trust, semantic alignment, and signal freshness that influences whether an LLM includes your brand in a generated answer. The overlap is real (strong topical authority correlates with ARM relevance), but the levers are different. ARM adds the entity registration and disambiguation layer that SEO tools do not measure, and it replaces rank tracking with citation frequency measurement.
Does ARM work for small brands and solo founders, or only enterprise?
ARM was designed with solo founders and small brands as the primary use case. Enterprise brands have existing authority footprints and large content teams — they do not need a framework to start from zero. Small brands do. The entity registration and disambiguation steps in ARM are particularly high-leverage for founders because a clean, unambiguous entity record in a small niche costs the same to build as it does for a large brand, but delivers proportionally greater citation lift because the competitive set is smaller.
How long before ARM implementation shows measurable citation results?
Authority signals take 60 to 90 days to propagate through the systems that matter — knowledge graphs update on weekly to monthly cycles, and LLM retrieval systems refresh at varying intervals. Relevance signals (content architecture and schema) can improve citation frequency faster, sometimes within 30 days, particularly in real-time retrieval systems like Perplexity. Momentum signals compound over 6 to 12 months. The ARM engagement is structured around a 90-day foundation phase and a 6-month compound phase, with citation frequency measured at both checkpoints.
Which AI systems does ARM optimize for?
The ARM Framework is designed to improve citation frequency across all major LLM-powered answer surfaces: ChatGPT (both trained knowledge and web retrieval), Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, Claude, Gemini, and emerging AI search interfaces. The signals that ARM builds — entity clarity, semantic precision, and fresh third-party citations — are input signals across all of these systems. There is no single-system optimization; ARM builds durable presence rather than gaming any individual retrieval architecture.

From there, scope and pricing depend on whether the primary constraint is authority (entity and schema work), relevance (content architecture), or momentum (editorial syndication).
