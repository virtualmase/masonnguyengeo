# UX, AI Visibility, SEO, and GEO Migration Standards

## Source record

The migration will use W3C’s WCAG 2.2 guidance as the baseline for accessible navigation, readable content, visible focus treatment, and predictable interaction behavior. The relevant reference surfaces are:

- W3C WCAG 2.2 Quick Reference: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/quickref/
- W3C guidance on navigable content: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/navigable.html
- W3C guidance on readable content: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/readable.html
- W3C ARIA Authoring Practices Guide: https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/

## Migration implication

Each migrated page or silo must preserve a clear page title, a visible location in navigation, keyboard-accessible controls, visible focus treatment, a skip path where navigation is repeated, and readable content structure. The migration should use semantic HTML before adding custom interaction patterns.

## Technical SEO and content-integrity references

The migration will use Google Search Central’s public guidance for crawlable architecture, people-first content, and structured-data integrity:

- Google SEO Starter Guide: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide
- Google Search Essentials: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials
- Google guidance on helpful, reliable, people-first content: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content
- Google general structured-data guidelines: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/sd-policies
- Google introduction to structured data: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/intro-structured-data

## SEO migration implication

Every migrated unit must retain or improve its canonical URL, unique title, unique description, semantic heading hierarchy, indexability intent, internal links, and sitemap presence. Structured data must describe visible, supportable content and may not contain placeholders, synthetic reviews, fabricated outcomes, or claims that the body does not substantiate. Content population will replace scaffold material only when a source, authorial basis, or explicit bounded status makes the new copy defensible.

## AI visibility and GEO references

The migration will use first-party Google guidance for AI features in Search and will not imply that any markup, wording, or file guarantees an AI citation:

- Google Search Central: AI Features and Your Website: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features
- Google Search Central: succeeding in AI Search: https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search

## AI visibility and GEO migration implication

The design goal is **retrieval readiness**, not a promised rank, citation, or answer-engine outcome. Each content unit must expose its main answer early, name the relevant entity consistently, use stable semantic HTML, preserve crawl access, align structured data with rendered content, and link to the nearest primary or supporting source.

Google’s first-party guidance states that AI features in Search have no special optimization, markup, or machine-readable-file requirement beyond its existing technical and people-first SEO foundations. Accordingly, `llms.txt` may function as a public reading index, but it is not treated as a crawler-control mechanism, a special AI-Search requirement, or proof of model ingestion. Every migrated page must be indexable and eligible for a normal Search snippet before any Google AI-feature visibility is possible; that eligibility still does not guarantee indexing, serving, or citation.

## AURE-informed palette study and adaptation boundary

The authorized AURE reference uses an institutional midnight base, archive ivory, cobalt-blue evidence signal, soft blue highlight, oxide-red risk marker, brass secondary accent, muted slate, and low-contrast structural rules. The public stylesheet is available at: https://github.com/Earthward-Holdings/AURE/blob/main/client/src/index.css

Mason Nguyen GEO will **not** duplicate AURE’s application layout, left audit rail, score instrument, locked-panel treatment, logo mark, or evidence-state semantics. It will adapt only the underlying color relationships: dark ultramarine as a deliberate reading ground, warm ivory as a high-legibility content field, one cobalt-blue action and focus signal, desaturated slate metadata, and restrained oxide or brass only for bounded warnings and citations. The personal research site remains editorial and source-led rather than product-audit UI.
